What AI Detectors Actually Look At

June 18, 2026

AI-detection tools don't "know" a document was written by a model. They estimate a probability based on statistical patterns that tend to differ between human and machine-generated text.

Perplexity

Perplexity measures how "surprised" a language model is by the next word in a sentence. Human writing tends to make occasional less-predictable word choices; heavily AI-generated text often picks the statistically safest word every time, which lowers perplexity and raises suspicion.

Burstiness

Burstiness looks at variation — in sentence length, structure, and rhythm — across a passage. Human writers naturally mix short punchy sentences with longer, more complex ones. Text that's unusually uniform in length and structure reads as more likely to be machine-generated.

Token-level patterns

  • Certain transition phrases and hedging constructions appear disproportionately in model output.
  • Overly consistent punctuation and paragraph shape can be a signal on their own.
  • No single signal is proof — detectors combine dozens of these into one probability score, which is why false positives on strong human writers do happen.
