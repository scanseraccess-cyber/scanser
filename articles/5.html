Reducing Plagiarism Without Losing Your Argument

June 25, 2026

There's a real difference between paraphrasing a source properly and simply disguising a match so a scanner doesn't catch it. Only one of these is defensible.

Paraphrasing, done right

A genuine paraphrase restates the idea using your own sentence structure and reasoning — not just synonyms — and is still followed by a citation, because the idea itself still belongs to the original author.

What to avoid

  • Swapping individual words while keeping the original sentence structure intact.
  • Paraphrasing so aggressively that the meaning drifts from the source you're citing.
  • Removing a citation because the wording no longer matches — the idea still needs attribution.

The better fix: cite it properly

If a passage is flagged and it genuinely is someone else's idea, the fix usually isn't to reword it until it's unrecognizable — it's to quote or cite it correctly. A well-cited paper with visible matches is stronger than one that has been rewritten just to avoid a scanner.
