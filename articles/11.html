Perplexity and Burstiness, Explained With Examples困惑度与突发性：举例说明

July 15, 20262026年7月15日

Two short passages make the difference concrete.

Example A — low burstiness

"The results were significant. The data supported the hypothesis. The method was effective. The conclusion followed logically." Every sentence is roughly the same length and shape — subject, verb, predictable adjective. This pattern reads as very uniform.

Example B — natural burstiness

"The results were significant, and the data lined up with what we'd predicted going in. It worked. What surprised us was how cleanly the conclusion followed once we saw the numbers." Sentence length varies, there's a short punch ("It worked."), and the phrasing takes a couple of small, less-predictable turns.

Why it matters

Neither passage is inherently "better" writing, but B carries more of the natural variation detectors associate with human authorship. Reading your own draft out loud is a surprisingly effective way to hear which version you've written.

两段简短的文字，可以让这种区别变得直观具体。

示例A——突发性较低

“结果具有显著性。数据支持了假设。该方法是有效的。结论也顺理成章。”每个句子的长度和结构几乎一样——主语、动词、可预测的形容词。这种模式读起来非常单一。

示例B——自然的突发性

“结果具有显著性，数据也和我们最初的预测相吻合。确实奏效了。真正让我们意外的是，看到数据之后，结论竟然如此顺理成章。”句子长度有变化，中间穿插了一个简短有力的短句（“确实奏效了。”），措辞也出现了几处不那么可预测的小转折。

为什么这一点很重要

这两段文字本身并无优劣之分，但示例B更多体现出检测工具通常与人类写作联系在一起的那种自然变化。把自己的草稿大声读出来，是判断你写的更接近哪一种的一个出乎意料有效的方法。
